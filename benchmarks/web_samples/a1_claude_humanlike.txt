Finding 3 — Monotonicity of bureau utilisation not enforced (Medium)
What we found
The GBM scorecard (XGBoost, 400 trees, depth 4) uses revolving utilisation from the bureau as its second most important feature by mean |SHAP|. When we plotted partial dependence on the validation sample, the score falls as expected from 0% to around 75% utilisation, but then rises again between roughly 85% and 95% before dropping off. In practice this means two otherwise identical applicants could get a better score at 92% utilisation than at 80%.
The developers told us this is a real pattern in the data — some high-utilisation customers are transactors who clear their balance each month — and that the effect is small. We looked at it. About 3.1% of approved applications in the last six months sit in the 85–95% band, so it's not negligible, and the reversal can't be explained to a declined customer in a way that would satisfy the adverse action notice requirements. We also re-ran the model with the monotone constraint on and the Gini dropped from 0.612 to 0.607 on the out-of-time sample, which is within noise.
Why it matters
Non-monotonic relationships on key bureau variables are hard to defend to customers and to the regulator, and the reversal is likely picking up a transactor effect that would be better modelled directly.
What we want done
1. Apply monotone constraints to utilisation and the two other bureau balance features flagged in Appendix B.
2. If the transactor effect is genuine, add an explicit transactor flag rather than letting the trees approximate it.
3. Re-run the reason-code mapping and confirm the top-4 reasons are stable for the 85–95% band.
