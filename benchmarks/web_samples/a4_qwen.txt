Methodology Section
Introduction
This methodology outlines the steps and processes involved in developing the Gradient Boosting Machine Learning Credit Card Application Scorecard. The objective is to create a robust and accurate predictor that assesses the risk associated with granting applications to new credit card holders. This approach leverages historical data from past transactions, account management activities, demographic information, and external factors such as socio-economic indicators.
Data Collection
The dataset used for this model was sourced through multiple channels including financial institution databases, public registries (such as Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection), third-party providers specializing in credit scoring, and social media analytics. Each source provided unique insights into various aspects of consumer behavior relevant to creditworthiness assessments. The data collected includes:
1. Demographic Information: Age, gender, income level, occupation.
2. Credit History: Payment history, balance due, total debt.
3. Transaction Patterns: Number of purchases per month, average transaction amount, frequency of transactions.
4. External Factors: Social media engagement, online activity metrics, employment status.
5. Geographical Details: Location, region of residence.
Data cleaning and preprocessing were applied to ensure accuracy and consistency across all variables. Missing values were imputed using median or mean techniques where appropriate, while outliers were handled according to statistical methods ensuring their relevance within the context of predictive modeling.
Model Development Process
1. Feature Engineering
- Categorical features were converted into numerical representations using encoding techniques like One-Hot Encoding and Label Encoding.
- Feature selection was performed using correlation analysis and mutual information, aiming to retain only those attributes that contribute significantly to predicting creditworthiness.
2. Model Selection
- Several Gradient Boosting algorithms were considered based on their performance characteristics: XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost. These were selected because they provide efficient handling of large datasets and can achieve very high prediction accuracy.
3. Training and Validation
- Splitting the data into training (70%) and validation sets (30%) helped maintain diversity across different segments of the population being analyzed. Cross-validation was used during the model tuning phase to evaluate its generalization ability.
