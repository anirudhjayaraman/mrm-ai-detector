# Model Limitations & Compensating Controls

## Introduction
This document outlines critical considerations regarding our Wholesale Loan-to-Default (LGD) models, which are used to assess potential losses from credit risk by analyzing historical data related to loan recovery rates after defaults have occurred. As with all predictive analytics tools, these models come with inherent limitations that must be acknowledged alongside effective control measures designed to mitigate risks associated with their deployment in financial decision-making processes. The following sections detail key constraints within the current LGD framework as well as appropriate strategic responses aimed at strengthening its reliability and integrity over time.

## Key Model Limitations

1. **Historical Data Bias**: Our prediction accuracy is heavily dependent upon past event trends; however, this approach can lead to biased results if there has been significant change or unforeseen events since those periods were recorded.
   - *Compensatory Measure*: Regularly update datasets including recent default instances while accounting for economic shifts affecting borrower behavior patterns. Perform stress testing under various hypothetical scenarios representing changes not reflected in existing history.
   
2. **Data Quality Issues**: Inconsistent record keeping across different loans might yield gaps or anomalies skewing predictions when nonlinear relationships exist between variables.
   - *Control Strategy*: Implement rigorous validation protocols ensuring high standards of cleanliness and completeness before feeding any dataset into the system. Utilize imputation methods judiciously wherever necessary but always flag areas requiring manual review due to discrepancies found during audits.

3. **Assumption Overhead**: Various assumptions embedded within statistical techniques – such as homoscedasticity among independent observations — may fail real-world scrutiny especially given complex interactions present amongst diverse portfolios' characteristics.
   - *Mitigation Action Plan*: Periodically reassess underlying distribution presumptions using diagnostic tests like Breusch-Pagan’s test. Explore alternative distributions more suitable than Gaussian ones considering empirical findings specific to customer segments we cater too extensively. Applying robust standard errors could help alleviate heteroskedastic effects without altering main structures significantly enough causing undue operational burden.
