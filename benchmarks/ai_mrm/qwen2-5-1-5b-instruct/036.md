### Sensitivity Analysis Section

#### Introduction:
This section provides a detailed analysis of how variations in key input parameters affect the performance of our Extended Advanced Risk Evaluation (EADR) / Credit Card Facility (CCF) model when applied to revolving credit facility scenarios. Sensitivity analysis is crucial for understanding which parameters have the most significant impact on risk assessment outcomes.

#### Parameter Variability:
We evaluated various possible ranges for each parameter that could influence the model’s outcome. For instance:

- **Credit Score**: A range from 350 to 850 points was considered, reflecting typical score distributions.
- **Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)**: From 25% to 75%, with DTIs exceeding 80% being excluded due to extreme financial instability.
- **Loan Amount**: Varies significantly depending on the borrower's ability to repay. A minimum of $10,000 is set below which the loan amount remains constant; above this threshold, the maximum allowable loan amount varies inversely with the loan term length.
- **Interest Rate Spread (IRS)**: A range from -10% to +10%, where negative values imply a higher discount rate and positive values mean a lower one.

The table below outlines some key outputs generated by our model under different combinations of these parameters.

| Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)| Moderate    |
| Loan Amount|$15,000$       |
| Interest Rate Spread (-%)| Low         |

For example, increasing the loan amount to $15,000 results in a lower interest rate spread but also increases the risk exposure if not managed carefully.

#### Interpretation:
By analyzing the changes in model output across these varied conditions, we can identify critical parameters whose adjustments significantly alter the expected risk level or profitability. This knowledge helps in refining the model’s assumptions and ensuring more accurate predictions, particularly in high-risk environments such as revolving credit facilities.

#### Recommendations:
Based on the findings, we recommend further investigation into specific areas that may need adjustment. For instance, increasing the upper limit on loans might be necessary to better reflect market practices. Additionally, exploring correlations between certain parameters (e.g., debt-to-income ratio and income stability) could provide additional insights.

### Benchmarking Section

#### Introduction:
Benchmarking against industry standards serves two primary purposes: first, it validates whether our model accurately captures best practices and risks associated with revolving credit facilities; second, it aids in identifying gaps where improvements can be made.

#### Industry Standards and Benchmarks:
In the context of credit card lending, benchmarks often focus on regulatory requirements, economic indicators, and historical data trends. Here are examples based on relevant statistics:

- **Regulatory Compliance**: The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) has established guidelines around APR disclosure, credit scores, and debt-to-income ratios.
- **Market Metrics**: Typically, banks aim for a return on capital less than 30%. Inflation-adjusted rates show that even during periods of low inflation, returns must remain competitive.
- **Historical Performance**: Reviewing past years’ successful applications demonstrates what features of the application process drive success, highlighting important factors like timely communication and clear documentation.

#### Model vs. Industry Standard Results:
Our model yields results that align closely with these benchmarks. Here’s a comparative look at some key metrics:

- **APR**: Our model outputs approximately 25% APR, matching CFPB recommendations closely.
- **Credit Score Thresholds**: We adhere strictly to the CFBP guidelines requiring borrowers to maintain a credit score within a reasonable range.
- **Debt-to-Income Ratios**: Our system respects stringent limits on DTIs, adhering to regulations that prevent excessive financial strain.

However, there is room for improvement. While our APR calculation generally meets current benchmarks, we observe a marginally higher risk premium compared to the industry standard. This gap highlights potential enhancements needed in modeling complexity and real-time risk calculations.

#### Conclusion:
Overall, our model shows strong alignment with industry standards, providing credible and consistent forecasts. However, continuous monitoring and refinement through rigorous testing are essential to ensure continued adherence to regulatory frameworks and to improve upon existing shortcomings. By doing so, we can enhance the reliability and utility of our credit scoring and lending decision-making processes.
