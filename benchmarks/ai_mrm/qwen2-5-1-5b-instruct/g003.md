### Supervisory Guidance on Expectations for a CVA/XVA Exposure Simulation Engine

**Supervisors' Attention**

1. **Accuracy and Reliability**: The CVA (Credit Value Adjustment) / XVA (Exposure at Default) exposure simulation engine must provide accurate and reliable results that reflect the true cost of credit risk associated with derivative contracts under the institution's existing portfolio. This includes ensuring that the model does not overestimate or underestimate the potential losses due to counterparty default.

2. **Model Validity**: Models used within the CVA/XVA exposure simulation engine should be based on sound economic principles and backed by robust empirical data or rigorous analytical techniques. Regulatory standards should support validation procedures such as sensitivity analysis, backtesting against historical market events, and stress testing scenarios representative of current and future economic conditions.

3. **Compliance with Standards**: All models employed must adhere to established regulatory guidelines and best practices. This encompasses compliance with international financial reporting standards (IFRS), relevant industry codes of conduct, and applicable local banking regulations related to valuation accounting and risk management.

4. **Risk Sensitivity Analysis**: The simulation engine should allow for flexibility in adjusting various input parameters to assess how changes in key assumptions affect the estimated exposures. This includes the ability to calibrate the model using different discount rates, correlation matrices, and other parameter values.

5. **Documentation and Reporting**: Comprehensive documentation should accompany all simulations, detailing methodologies, assumptions, and inputs used. Regular reports should be generated to summarize findings, highlighting significant deviations between modeled and actual outcomes and any identified risks or vulnerabilities.

6. **Operational Independence**: The development, implementation, and maintenance of the CVA/XVA exposure simulation engine must maintain operational independence from investment banks or proprietary trading units, which can lead to biased or inconsistent modeling results.

7. **Technology Updates and Maintenance**: Ongoing technological advancements should drive continuous updates to the simulation software to ensure it remains current and effective. Regular updates should address new types of derivatives, emerging markets, and changing risk profiles, while also addressing any issues reported through user feedback or audits.

8. **Transparency and Communication**: Stakeholders, including regulators, counterparties, and internal auditors, should have access to clear and transparent information regarding the use and results of the CVA/XVA exposure simulation engine. Transparent communication helps build trust and ensures that decisions made based on these simulations align with broader regulatory objectives.

By adhering to these expectations, institutions can enhance their risk management capabilities, promote greater transparency, and contribute to the overall stability and resilience of the financial system. Regulatory authorities encourage entities to continuously review and improve their systems to stay ahead of evolving risk landscapes and comply with evolving regulatory requirements.
