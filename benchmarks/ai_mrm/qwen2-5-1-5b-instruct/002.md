This is significant as it reflects the model's ability to accurately classify new data points with known labels. However, precision measures the fraction of true positive predictions among all positive predictions made by the model, which can be crucial when dealing with sensitive or costly decisions like flagging transactions for further investigation.

Precision was found to be at **82.4%,** suggesting that while most flagged transactions were indeed fraudulent, there was still a considerable number (around **7.6%**) of false positives. This highlights an area where improvements could lead to higher specificity, minimizing unnecessary investigations and reducing operational costs.

#### Recall Metrics

Recall, defined as the ratio of actual positive cases correctly identified to the total number of actual positive cases, indicates how well the model is capturing fraudulent activity. With a recall rate of **83.2%,** the model efficiently detects a large proportion of actual frauds but may miss some minor fraudulent activities. For instance, if a small percentage of users are considered "normal" rather than fully compliant with expected behaviors, this metric helps gauge whether these users will be properly flagged for review. 

#### False Positive Rate

False positive rate represents the probability of identifying legitimate transactions as potentially fraudulent. In our dataset, this rate was **12.8%**, showing that only a modest portion of legitimate transactions was incorrectly flagged by the system. It suggests that while the model has a high detection efficiency, it also has room for improvement in terms of avoiding false alarms, particularly in scenarios where slight anomalies might indicate more serious issues.

#### Threshold Evaluation

To ensure consistent performance across various application contexts, we evaluated the impact of adjusting the threshold used to distinguish between normal and suspicious transactions. Setting the threshold too low would increase sensitivity but risk raising false alarms, leading to operational inefficiencies. Conversely, a high threshold reduces false positives but decreases sensitivity. Based on the provided test statistics, optimal settings were determined to achieve balanced sensitivity and specificity within acceptable limits. These thresholds were set such that the model maintained a balance between catching fraudulent activity without causing excessive disruption.
