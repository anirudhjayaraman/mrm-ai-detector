**Outcomes Analysis and Back-Testing Section**

The objectives of this backtesting exercise were to evaluate the accuracy of our expected credit loss (ECL) model under various market conditions and to identify any deviations from anticipated results. The backtesting process was designed to ensure that the model's assumptions remained consistent with those used during the initial development phase.

### Market Scenario Outcomes

1. **High Inflation Environment:**
   - During this scenario, we observed significant increases in interest rates and commodity prices.
   - The ECL projections showed a substantial decline in provisioning ratios due to higher-than-expected defaults on loans tied to commodities.
   - This outcome suggests that the inflation sensitivity of loan losses is accurately captured, as predicted.

2. **Low Economic Growth Scenario:**
   - With low economic growth, we noted reduced demand for goods and services across industries, leading to lower default probabilities on consumer loans.
   - Provisions increased accordingly, indicating that the model correctly anticipated the impact of slower economic activity on loan delinquencies.
   
3. **Rising Interest Rates Scenario:**
   - As interest rates began to rise, it led to a more conservative approach towards provisioning, reflecting increased uncertainty among borrowers regarding future borrowing capabilities.
   - Results indicated a more cautious provisioning behavior compared to historical norms, confirming the robustness of the model’s risk assessment.

4. **Slowing Global Trade:**
   - A reduction in global trade volumes significantly impacted manufacturing sector default rates.
   - The model forecasted these declines effectively, showing a marked decrease in provisioning expectations compared to baseline forecasts.

### Macroeconomic Sensitivity

To assess the model’s sensitivity to broader macroeconomic factors, we employed stress tests incorporating multiple variables such as GDP growth rates, unemployment levels, and exchange rate fluctuations.

- **GDP Growth Stress Test:** Elevated or depressed GDP growth rates resulted in varied but predictable changes in default probabilities, aligning well with theoretical considerations.
- **Unemployment Rate Adjustments:** Higher unemployment rates corresponded with heightened expectations of borrower difficulties in repaying debts.
- **Exchange Rate Fluctuations:** Significant movements in currency values affected lending activities globally, influencing cross-border transactions and thus default risks.

These results demonstrated strong internal consistency between macroeconomic inputs and the output of the ECL model, ensuring accurate predictions within the tested parameter ranges.

### Limitations and Areas for Improvement

Despite achieving high levels of accuracy, there remain certain limitations and areas for improvement:

1. **Seasonal Variability:** While models often incorporate seasonal adjustments, variations still exist depending on local economies and sectors.
2. **Operational Costs and Uncertainty Management:** Managing operational costs can influence provisioning decisions; future enhancements should focus on refining cost structures and operational efficiency.
3. **Model Complexity:** The inclusion of complex micro-economic models may introduce additional sources of variability, necessitating ongoing calibration and refinement.

### Conclusion

This back-testing exercise successfully validated our IFRS 9 ECL model against diverse market conditions and macroeconomic scenarios. The outcomes supported the robustness of our model’s assumptions and provided insights into its effectiveness. Moving forward, improvements will be made to address identified limitations, enhancing the model's overall accuracy and reliability in real-world applications.
