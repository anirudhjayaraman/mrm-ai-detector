**Sensitivity Analysis and Benchmarking Section**

To ensure that our vendor commercial real estate default model accurately reflects the underlying probabilities associated with borrower defaults, we have conducted rigorous sensitivity analysis and compared it against industry benchmarks to achieve confidence in its predictive capabilities.

### Sensitivity Analysis

Our model employs various financial metrics to assess the likelihood of borrowers defaulting on their loan obligations. The primary inputs used include:

1. **Credit Scores:** We utilize Standard & Poor's (S&P) credit scores as a key indicator, which range from 450 (lowest risk) to 750 (highest risk). Credit score distributions have been normalized using historical data to reflect current market conditions.
   
2. **Leverage Ratios:** Borrowers' leverage ratios are calculated based on debt-to-equity and total-liabilities-to-total-assets metrics, adjusted for potential liquidity constraints and future refinancing options.

3. **Repayment Capacity:** This is assessed through an algorithmic valuation approach utilizing cash flow projections, asset valuations, and income statements, adjusted for anticipated delinquency rates and projected recovery amounts.

The outcomes derived from this analysis are evaluated under different scenarios to identify thresholds where significant changes in input values might impact the model’s output probability distribution significantly. Specifically, we analyze how slight variations in these inputs affect the predicted default probability across all categories of loans within the portfolio. The results help us understand the relative sensitivities of each component to changes in assumptions.

For instance, a 1% increase in the expected loss rate due to higher than average interest rates could lead to a 3-5 percentage point shift in the overall probability of default. Conversely, a 10% reduction in the average recovery ratio would result in a 2-3 percentage point decrease in the default probability.

### Benchmarking Against Industry Standards

Benchmarking serves multiple purposes: it helps validate the accuracy of our model against recognized industry standards, ensuring compliance with best practices; it provides insights into the competitive landscape; and it highlights opportunities for improvement or differentiation based on performance metrics.

We compare our model’s outputs to widely accepted benchmarks such as Moody’s Analytics Commercial Real Estate Risk Model, JPMorgan Chase’s Credit Metrics model, and Fitch Ratings’ methodology. Key metrics analyzed include default probabilities, recovery rates, and loan-to-value ratios. 

Through comparative analyses, we observe that our model generally aligns closely with industry averages but exhibits slightly more conservative estimates. However, deviations indicate areas requiring further refinement. For example, our estimated default probability for high-risk segments tends to be slightly lower, reflecting potentially more optimistic economic forecasts versus broader sector trends observed in peer models.

This alignment underscores the robustness of our model while highlighting necessary adjustments to enhance model consistency with industry norms.

### Conclusion

By conducting comprehensive sensitivity analysis and benchmarking exercises, we demonstrate the reliability and validity of our commercial real estate default model. These methodologies not only provide assurance regarding the model’s operational effectiveness but also inform continuous improvement processes aimed at refining our model’s accuracy and applicability to diverse markets and scenarios.
