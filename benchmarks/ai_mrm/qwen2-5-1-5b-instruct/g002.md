### Finding Report: Inspectors General Audit of Wholesale Lender Guarantor Debt (LGD) Model Oversight and Workout Recoveries

---

#### **Executive Summary**

The Inspector General has conducted an in-depth review of the Wholesale Lender Guarantor Debt (LGD) model developed and maintained by the Wholesale Credit Risk Management Department within the Banking Regulatory Authority. The focus of this investigation was to assess whether there were any significant oversights or lapses in management practices that could impact the accuracy and reliability of the LGD model used during workout recovery procedures.

This report highlights key findings, areas of concern, and recommendations aimed at ensuring transparency, accountability, and robust risk assessment methodologies.

---

#### **1. Introduction**

The Wholesale Credit Risk Management Department is responsible for overseeing the development, implementation, and maintenance of various credit risk models within the regulatory framework. One such critical tool is the LGD model, which estimates the expected loss components of loans when assets are written down through workouts.

The objective of this audit was to determine if there were any procedural deficiencies or errors in the LGD model’s application, particularly focusing on workout recovery scenarios. The findings aim to provide insights into best practices for LGD modeling and ensure consistent application across different institutions under similar circumstances.

---

#### **2. Methodology**

The inspection process included:

- **Review of Documentation**: Examination of internal policies, guidelines, and reports related to LGD model usage.
- **Model Validation**: Analysis of historical data comparisons with actual workout outcomes.
- **Interviews with Key Personnel**: Gathering qualitative feedback on model performance and adherence to established standards.
- **Statistical Analysis**: Comparing simulated workout outcomes against observed recovery rates to identify discrepancies.

---

#### **3. Findings**

**3.1 Accuracy and Consistency of LGD Modeling**

Upon reviewing the documentation, it was evident that while the LGD model had been successfully implemented, some inconsistencies were noted regarding its application during workout recovery periods. Specifically, several instances where the model overestimated losses were identified due to outdated assumptions and flawed methodology.

For example, one scenario involved a loan restructuring that led to a significant reduction in principal but resulted in higher-than-expected losses according to the LGD model. This discrepancy highlighted the need for continuous refinement and updating of input parameters such as default probabilities and loss given defaults.

**3.2 Data Quality Concerns**

Inspection also uncovered issues with the quality of underlying data utilized in the LGD model. Historical datasets often contained inaccuracies or biases that skewed predicted values. For instance, missing information on borrower characteristics like income levels or asset values affected both the validity and relevance of estimated losses.

Moreover, the lack of real-time monitoring of market conditions meant that certain inputs needed regular recalibration to reflect current economic trends accurately.

**3.3 Ongoing Revalidation Processes**

While the LGD model was generally sound, the frequency of revalidation checks did not meet industry best practices. In many cases, model updates required manual intervention rather than automated processes, leading to delays in applying new risk assessments. Additionally, reviews were infrequent enough to overlook changes in financial regulations and operational strategies affecting risk profiles.

**3.4 Interdepartmental Communication Challenges**

Communication gaps between departments, particularly those specializing in lending operations versus credit risk management, contributed significantly to inconsistent LGD model applications. Misaligned expectations and unclear reporting mechanisms hindered effective coordination during workouts, causing confusion and suboptimal outcomes.

---

#### **4. Areas for Improvement**

To address these concerns, the following measures should be considered:

- **Enhance Regular Revalidation Procedures:** Implement standardized revalidation protocols for the LGD model, incorporating automated systems for quicker adjustments based on evolving economic indicators and regulatory frameworks.

- **Foster Collaboration Across Departments:** Promote joint training sessions and cross-departmental collaboration to harmonize understanding and communication around LGD model usage and interpretation.

- **Improve Data Quality Controls:** Establish robust validation pipelines to ensure high-quality, timely data entry and thorough auditing of data integrity before deployment.

- **Strengthen Internal Review Mechanisms:** Enhance the frequency and comprehensiveness of internal audits to catch anomalies early and prevent them from escalating to more significant impacts later on.
