The rapid expansion of financial services in the retail sector has necessitated sophisticated models to assess credit risk accurately. In this context, logistic regression models have been widely utilized within Retail Mortgage Processing Decision Scorecards (PD scores), which serve as predictive tools to determine loan approval probabilities. However, these models can be susceptible to errors or biases known as **model risk**, particularly when dealing with unrepresentative data sets or assumptions that do not fully capture the complexity of real-world economic conditions.

### Study Overview

This research delves into the potential vulnerabilities of Logistic Regression models employed in Retail Mortgage PD scorecards, specifically focusing on the issue of model risk. Our study aims to provide actionable insights by quantifying the impact of various factors influencing credit risk assessment through detailed analysis of a large-scale dataset from an insurance company. The dataset includes historical information on thousands of mortgage applications, categorized by their final status – approved or declined.

### Methodology

1. **Data Collection**: We collected a comprehensive database spanning over five years, including customer demographics, income levels, employment status, home values, debt ratios, and past loan defaults. This dataset was meticulously cleaned and preprocessed, ensuring robustness against missing values and outliers.

2. **Model Development**: Using the cleaned dataset, we built multiple logistic regression models tailored for predicting whether a mortgage application would be approved or denied based on provided features. Each model was independently trained and validated using cross-validation techniques to ensure reliability across different subsets of the data.

3. **Performance Metrics**: To evaluate the accuracy and interpretability of our models, we calculated key performance metrics such as Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, Area Under ROC Curve (AUC-ROC), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). These metrics were crucial in identifying areas where the models showed significant deviations from expected outcomes.

4. **Threshold Analysis**: Through a thorough analysis of decision rules derived from each model, we examined the relationship between threshold settings and the proportion of true positives and negatives among those predicted to approve loans. This revealed how different cut-off points could affect the overall risk profile assessed by the system.

5. **Validation Across Different Time Periods**: To gauge the consistency of our findings across varying market conditions, we analyzed the models' predictions over distinct time windows – from initial application submissions to subsequent refinancing periods. This method allowed us to identify trends and anomalies that might indicate model drift or adaptation issues.

6. **Statistical Testing**: For rigorous validation, we conducted statistical tests at both the mean level and percentile-based intervals to detect any systematic bias in the model’s outputs. These tests included t-tests, ANOVA, and chi-square analyses to ensure that observed differences did not occur by chance.

7. **Conclusion and Recommendations**: Based on our empirical findings, we proposed recommendations aimed at enhancing the robustness and fairness of Retail Mortgage PD scorecards. Potential strategies involved refining model parameters to improve generalizability, incorporating additional variables relevant to diverse market segments, and regular monitoring and updating of models to align them more closely with evolving regulatory standards.

### Conclusion

Our study underscores the critical importance of maintaining vigilance against model risk in Retail Mortgage PD scorecards. By systematically evaluating the performance, thresholds, and external validity of existing models, we aim to contribute valuable insights that will help stakeholders enhance the reliability and accountability of credit risk assessments in the financial industry. Future work may explore the integration of machine learning techniques alongside traditional regression methods, further enriching the analytical landscape for predictive modeling in retail lending scenarios.
