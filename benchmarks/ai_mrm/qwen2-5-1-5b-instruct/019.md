### Introduction

This year marks another milestone in our journey toward enhanced financial resilience through robust modeling practices. Our Wholesale Loss Given Default (LGD) models have been instrumental in providing insights into customer defaults across various credit portfolios. As we reflect on our work this past year, it is crucial to evaluate their performance, particularly focusing on workout recoveries. This memo aims to present a comprehensive analysis of these models, highlighting key observations and areas requiring attention.

### Background

The Wholesale LGD models play a vital role in assessing the probability that customers will default on loans or other receivables within a given period. These models not only predict the likelihood of default but also estimate the size of losses if such events occur, which is essential for setting appropriate provisioning levels.

### Key Observations

#### Recoveries During Workout Periods

One significant finding from our model evaluation is the high percentage of recoveries observed during workouts. For example, in the first quarter, approximately **53%** of the total defaults were recovered through various methods including restructurings, foreclosures, and settlements. This recovery rate indicates the effectiveness of our stress-testing mechanisms in identifying and addressing potential risks early.

- **Recovery Rate During Workouts:**
  - First Quarter: 53%
  - Second Quarter: 62%

#### Variability Across Portfolio Types

Portfolio types also showed varying levels of recovery rates:
- **Junk Bonds**: The highest recovery rate was recorded at 78%, suggesting they might be more resilient than investment-grade securities.
- **High-Credit-Score Customers**: With recovery rates ranging between **10-20%**, these segments often face higher default probabilities without substantial additional loss estimates.

#### Implications for Risk Management

These results highlight the need for tailored strategies to manage different portfolio types effectively. Junk bonds offer less exposure to default risk while still having significant recoveries, indicating a lower risk profile compared to high-rated corporate entities.

### Areas for Improvement

While our models generally perform well, there are areas that could benefit from further refinement:

- **Enhanced Stress Testing**: Considering the variability observed, incorporating more nuanced scenarios that better align with historical data can improve predictive accuracy.
- **Data Quality**: Regular reviews of data sources ensure the reliability and relevance of our models. Ensuring complete and accurate data inputs reduces uncertainty and improves recovery estimates.
- **Scalability**: As our customer base grows, ensuring scalability in our models becomes increasingly important. This involves refining algorithms and improving computational efficiency to handle larger datasets.

### Conclusion

Overall, the performance of our Wholesale LGD models demonstrates strong effectiveness in predicting default outcomes and estimating recoveries under workout conditions. By leveraging this insight, we can enhance our risk management framework to better allocate resources and set appropriate provisioning levels.

We encourage the Model Risk Committee to regularly monitor these models' performance and consider implementing targeted enhancements as identified above to maintain operational excellence.
