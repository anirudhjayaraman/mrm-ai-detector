**Supervisory Guidance on Historical-Simulation-Based Value-at-Risk Models for the Trading Book**

### Introduction

The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) has mandated that banks implement sophisticated risk management tools to monitor and control their trading book risks more effectively. Historically-based simulation models have emerged as robust methods due to their ability to capture market dynamics over extended periods. This document outlines supervisory expectations for the implementation and ongoing monitoring of these models within the context of the trading book.

### Core Expectations

1. **Model Development and Validation**
   - Ensure that the historical-simulation model is thoroughly developed using a sufficient number of past data points representative of historical volatility and correlation patterns.
   - Validate the model against known scenarios through sensitivity analyses and stress testing to ensure its reliability in extreme conditions.

2. **Parameterization**
   - Parameters such as the mean reversion period, volatilities, and correlation matrices should be estimated with high precision and calibrated iteratively until they reflect current market realities accurately.
   - Use advanced statistical techniques like principal component analysis (PCA) or factor analysis to reduce dimensionality without sacrificing predictive power.

3. **Risk Metrics Reporting**
   - Regularly report the VaR (Value at Risk) calculated under both the Monte Carlo simulation approach and historical simulation method. The difference between these two approaches should be small to confirm the consistency of model outputs.
   - Present the VaR along with confidence intervals or coverage probabilities to provide a comprehensive view of potential losses.

4. **Scenario Analysis**
   - Conduct scenario simulations to test how different types of market events could affect the portfolio values over time.
   - Consider various stress tests including interest rate changes, equity price movements, liquidity shocks, etc., to understand the robustness of the model against hypothetical crises.

5. **Documentation and Training**
   - Maintain thorough documentation explaining the assumptions, methodologies, calibration procedures, and any other critical parameters used in the VaR calculation.
   - Provide regular training sessions for traders and risk managers to keep them updated with new developments in econometrics and financial modeling.

### Quantitative Requirements

To meet these requirements, we propose adhering to the following guidelines:

- **Data Sample Size:** At least five years of daily data with full records of every trade executed within each session (excluding holidays).
- **Volatility Estimation:** Daily implied volatility curves derived from option prices or basket indices covering at least one standard deviation per quarter, adjusted monthly according to recent performance trends.
- **Correlation Matrix Updates:** Quarterly updates based on industry-standard methods ensuring all correlations remain up-to-date with market shifts.

### Monitoring Framework

Implement a dynamic monitoring framework leveraging technology-driven solutions for continuous real-time updates and alerts. Key metrics include:
- **VaR Coverage Rates:** Consistently above 98% across all significant trades, indicating strong confidence levels even during stressed conditions.
- **Performance Against Baseline:** VaRs calculated using different models compared side-by-side to highlight discrepancies and validate the effectiveness of historical simulation.

By adhering to these detailed guidelines and continuously refining the methodology, institutions can enhance their ability to manage trading book risks prudently, thereby supporting sound capital allocation decisions and overall stability within the banking sector.
