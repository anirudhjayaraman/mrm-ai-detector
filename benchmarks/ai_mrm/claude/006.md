# Effective Challenge Under SR 11-7

## Introduction

Effective challenge is a cornerstone of the model risk management framework set out in the Federal Reserve's SR 11-7 guidance. The guidance defines effective challenge as the "critical analysis by objective, informed parties who can identify model limitations and assumptions and produce appropriate changes." This document describes how the Bank ensures that effective challenge is embedded throughout the model lifecycle.

## The Three Elements of Effective Challenge

SR 11-7 identifies three conditions that must be present for challenge to be effective: incentives, competence, and influence.

### Incentives

Challenge is more effective when the challengers have appropriate incentives to identify problems. The Bank supports this by maintaining an independent Model Risk Management (MRM) function that reports to the Chief Risk Officer rather than to the business lines whose models it validates. Validator compensation is not linked to the performance of the business units they review, and validation outcomes are not subject to approval by model owners.

### Competence

Effective challenge requires that challengers have the technical knowledge and experience necessary to understand the models they review. The Bank's MRM team includes staff with advanced degrees in quantitative disciplines, along with experienced practitioners who have worked in model development roles. The Bank invests in ongoing training and maintains a skills matrix to ensure appropriate coverage across model types, including credit, market, operational risk, and machine learning models.

### Influence

Challenge must be able to produce change. The Bank ensures this through clear escalation channels and governance authority. MRM has the authority to restrict or prohibit model use, impose conditions of use, and require remediation within set timeframes. Findings are tracked in the Bank's issue management system and reported monthly to the Model Risk Committee, with overdue high-severity items escalated to the Board Risk Committee.

## Effective Challenge Across the Model Lifecycle

Effective challenge is not limited to the formal validation process. The Bank embeds it at multiple stages:

- **Development:** Peer reviews within the first line provide initial challenge before submission to MRM.
- **Validation:** MRM conducts independent testing, including replication, benchmarking, and sensitivity analysis.
- **Use:** Model users are expected to understand model limitations and raise concerns when outputs appear inconsistent with business intuition.
- **Internal Audit:** As the third line of defence, Internal Audit periodically assesses whether the MRM framework operates effectively.

## Evidence of Effective Challenge

To demonstrate compliance, the Bank maintains documentation showing that challenge has taken place and has had an impact. This includes validation reports with documented findings, records of developer responses and remediation, minutes of Model Risk Committee meetings, and instances where model use was restricted or models were redeveloped as a result of MRM challenge.

## Conclusion

By ensuring that challengers have the right incentives, competence, and influence, and by embedding challenge throughout the model lifecycle, the Bank maintains a robust approach to effective challenge that is consistent with the expectations of SR 11-7.
