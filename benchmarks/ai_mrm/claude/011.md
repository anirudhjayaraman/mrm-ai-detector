## 4. Data Quality and Representativeness

### 4.1 Data Sources

The LGD model is developed using the Bank's internal workout database, which records recoveries, costs, and cure outcomes for defaulted wholesale exposures. The development sample covers defaults between January 2008 and December 2021, comprising 1,284 defaulted facilities across 912 obligors. Supplementary data on collateral values is sourced from the collateral management system, and macroeconomic indicators are obtained from external providers.

### 4.2 Data Quality Assessment

The validation team performed a comprehensive review of data quality across the dimensions of completeness, accuracy, consistency, and timeliness.

**Completeness:** Overall completeness of key fields is high. However, collateral valuation dates are missing for 14% of secured facilities defaulting before 2012. The developers imputed these using the facility origination date, which is likely to overstate the age of the valuation and therefore understate collateral coverage.

**Accuracy:** We sampled 60 defaulted facilities and reconciled recovery cash flows to source systems. Fifty-six reconciled within tolerance. The four exceptions related to recoveries received in foreign currencies, which were converted at the default-date exchange rate rather than the date of receipt. The impact on realised LGD was less than 1 percentage point in each case.

**Consistency:** The default definition changed in 2019 following the implementation of the EBA Guidelines on the definition of default. The developers have applied the new definition retrospectively to the extent possible, but for defaults before 2015, the counting of days past due for materiality thresholds cannot be fully reconstructed.

**Timeliness:** Workouts that remain open at the observation date are included using the treatment described in Section 3.5. Incomplete workouts represent 9% of the sample.

### 4.3 Representativeness

The representativeness of the development sample relative to the current portfolio was assessed across key risk dimensions, including industry sector, geography, seniority, collateral type, and exposure size.

The sample is broadly representative of the current portfolio. Notable differences include:

- Commercial real estate exposures represent 31% of the current portfolio but only 19% of the development defaults.
- Facilities above €50m are underrepresented, with only 23 defaults in the sample.
- The share of unsecured exposures has fallen from 42% at the start of the sample to 28% in the current portfolio.

The underrepresentation of large exposures and commercial real estate is a concern given the materiality of these segments. The developers have addressed this partly through segmentation, but the CRE segment relies on 244 observations, which limits the precision of the estimates.

### 4.4 Conclusion

Data quality is generally adequate for model development. The validation team raises Finding 3 regarding the imputation of collateral valuation dates and Finding 4 regarding the representativeness of CRE and large exposures, and recommends that the developers quantify the potential impact through sensitivity analysis.
