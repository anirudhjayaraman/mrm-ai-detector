Dear Model Risk Management Policy Team,

Response to Consultation Paper: Model risk management principles for banks

The Association welcomes the opportunity to respond to the Prudential Regulation Authority's consultation on model risk management principles for banks. Our members, which include large internationally active banks as well as smaller domestic lenders, are strongly supportive of the PRA's objective of establishing a clear and consistent set of expectations for the management of model risk. We believe that a principles-based approach is appropriate, and we welcome the PRA's recognition of proportionality.

We would, however, like to raise a number of points where we believe further clarity or refinement would be beneficial.

1. Definition of a model

The proposed definition of a model is broad and, in our view, risks capturing a large number of simple calculation tools that do not give rise to meaningful model risk. Several members have estimated that applying the definition as drafted would increase the size of their model inventories by between 50% and 200%, with a corresponding increase in validation workload. We would encourage the PRA to clarify that firms may apply a materiality threshold when determining which deterministic quantitative methods should be included in the inventory, and that such tools need not be subject to the full set of model risk management requirements.

2. Model risk appetite

We support the expectation that firms should set a model risk appetite. However, we note that there is no established industry methodology for quantifying aggregate model risk. We would welcome confirmation that firms may use a combination of qualitative statements and quantitative indicators, such as the number of models with overdue validations or open high-severity findings, rather than a single quantitative measure.

3. Senior Manager accountability

The proposal to assign responsibility for the model risk management framework to a single Senior Management Function holder is reasonable. However, members would welcome clarification that this accountability relates to the overall framework, rather than to individual models, for which responsibility should remain with model owners.

4. Implementation timeline

Given the scale of the changes required, particularly in relation to the expansion of the model inventory and the development of model risk appetite metrics, we believe that the proposed twelve-month implementation period is challenging. We would encourage the PRA to consider a phased implementation over at least twenty-four months.

We would be happy to discuss any of these points in further detail.

Yours faithfully,

Director, Prudential Policy
