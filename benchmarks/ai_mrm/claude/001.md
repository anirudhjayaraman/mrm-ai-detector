## 3. Conceptual Soundness

### 3.1 Overview

The Retail Mortgage PD Scorecard estimates the probability that an obligor will default within a 12-month horizon. The model uses a logistic regression framework, which is a well-established and widely accepted approach for binary default prediction in retail credit portfolios. The validation team assessed whether the theoretical foundations, modelling choices, and key assumptions are appropriate for the model's intended use in IRB capital calculation.

### 3.2 Model Design and Methodology

Logistic regression is an appropriate choice for this application. It produces outputs that are directly interpretable as probabilities, its coefficients can be explained to business users and regulators, and it is consistent with industry practice for retail mortgage portfolios. The developers considered alternative approaches, including gradient-boosted trees and survival models, and documented their rationale for selecting logistic regression on the basis of interpretability, stability, and regulatory acceptance.

The validation team agrees with this assessment. While machine learning techniques may offer marginal gains in discriminatory power, the transparency of logistic regression is a significant advantage in an IRB context, where model outputs drive capital requirements and must withstand supervisory scrutiny.

### 3.3 Variable Selection

The final model includes eight risk drivers spanning application characteristics, behavioural information, and collateral metrics, including current loan-to-value, months in arrears over the last 12 months, debt-to-income ratio, and bureau delinquency indicators. The variable selection process combined statistical techniques—univariate Gini analysis, information value screening, and stepwise selection—with expert judgement from the business.

Overall, the selected variables are intuitive and exhibit the expected directional relationships with default risk. However, the validation team notes the following:

- **Correlation between drivers:** Current LTV and the indexed property value variable show a correlation of 0.71. While the variance inflation factors remain below commonly used thresholds, the developers should monitor this relationship over time.
- **Exclusion of macroeconomic variables:** The model is designed as a through-the-cycle rating system, and the exclusion of macroeconomic drivers is consistent with this philosophy. Cyclicality is instead addressed through the calibration to long-run average default rates.

### 3.4 Key Assumptions

The model relies on several key assumptions, including the stability of the relationship between risk drivers and default over time, the representativeness of the development sample, and the appropriateness of the 90 days past due default definition. These assumptions are clearly documented and reasonable. The validation team recommends that the developers strengthen the documentation supporting the representativeness assumption, particularly with respect to the interest-only segment, which has grown materially since the development window.

### 3.5 Conclusion

The validation team concludes that the model is conceptually sound and fit for its intended purpose, subject to the remediation of Finding 2 (documentation of representativeness for the interest-only segment).
