# EAD Model for Revolving Credit Facilities — Development Summary

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document summarises the development of the Exposure at Default (EAD) model for revolving retail credit facilities, including credit cards and personal overdrafts. The model estimates the credit conversion factor (CCF) applied to the undrawn portion of committed limits in order to calculate EAD for IRB regulatory capital purposes. It replaces the existing model, which was developed in 2016 and no longer meets the requirements of the EBA Guidelines on PD and LGD estimation and the PRA's expectations for IRB models.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Modelling Approach

The model follows the fixed-horizon approach, in which EAD is observed at the point of default and the reference date is set 12 months prior. The CCF is defined as:

CCF = (EAD − Drawn₀) / (Limit₀ − Drawn₀)

where Drawn₀ and Limit₀ are the drawn balance and limit at the reference date.

Because CCFs are bounded and exhibit a bimodal distribution, with significant mass at 0 and 1, a direct linear regression is not appropriate. Instead, the model uses a two-stage approach:

1. **Segmentation:** Facilities are grouped into homogeneous pools based on product type, utilisation at reference date, and months on book.
2. **Pool-level estimation:** For each pool, the long-run average CCF is estimated as the exposure-weighted average of observed CCFs across the observation period.

### 2.2 Segmentation

Segmentation was determined using a decision tree combined with expert judgement. Utilisation at the reference date was found to be the most significant driver, with low-utilisation accounts showing materially higher CCFs. The final segmentation comprises eight pools.

## 3. Data

The development sample includes 48,600 defaulted facilities observed between 2009 and 2023. Data quality checks were performed on all key variables, and exclusions were applied for facilities with data errors or limits changed during the observation period.

## 4. Calibration and Conservatism

The long-run average CCF for each pool is calculated over the full observation period, which includes the 2009–2010 downturn. A downturn adjustment is applied in accordance with the EBA RTS on downturn conditions. A margin of conservatism is added to address identified deficiencies, including data gaps for pre-2012 defaults and uncertainty in the treatment of limit reductions.

## 5. Model Performance

The model shows good rank-ordering and calibration performance across pools. Back-testing against out-of-time data indicates that predicted CCFs are conservative relative to observed outcomes in all pools.

## 6. Limitations

The main limitations of the model include the use of pooled estimates that do not capture account-level behaviour, the limited number of defaults in some pools, and the reliance on historical behaviour that may not reflect future changes in customer or bank behaviour such as proactive limit management.

## 7. Conclusion

The model provides a robust and conservative approach to EAD estimation that meets regulatory requirements and is suitable for use in IRB capital calculations.
