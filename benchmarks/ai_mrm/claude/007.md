# 4. Model Methodology

## 4.1 Model Dynamics

The model describes the joint evolution of the underlying equity price S and its instantaneous variance v under the risk-neutral measure:

dS_t = (r_t − q_t) S_t dt + √v_t S_t dW_t^S
dv_t = κ(θ − v_t) dt + ξ √v_t dW_t^v
d⟨W^S, W^v⟩_t = ρ dt

where κ is the speed of mean reversion, θ the long-run variance, ξ the volatility of variance, and ρ the correlation between spot and variance shocks. Deterministic interest rates and dividend yields are taken from the bank's curve and dividend infrastructure.

The Heston model was selected because it captures two features of equity option markets that are material for the products in scope: the negative skew of implied volatility, driven primarily by ρ, and the stochastic evolution of forward volatility, which matters for path-dependent payoffs such as cliquets and autocallables. Unlike local volatility, the model produces realistic forward smile dynamics, which is essential for the pricing of forward-starting options.

## 4.2 Calibration

The five parameters (v₀, κ, θ, ξ, ρ) are calibrated daily to the listed vanilla option surface for each underlying by minimising a weighted sum of squared differences between model and market implied volatilities:

min Σ_i w_i (σ_i^model − σ_i^market)²

Weights are set proportional to vega and inversely proportional to bid–ask spread, so that liquid near-the-money options dominate the fit. Vanilla prices are computed semi-analytically using the characteristic function and the Carr–Madan Fourier transform, with the "little Heston trap" formulation to avoid branch-cut discontinuities in the complex logarithm.

The optimisation uses a Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm initialised from the previous day's parameters. If the calibration error exceeds 1.5 volatility points RMSE, the procedure is restarted from a grid of 50 initial points and the best solution retained. The Feller condition (2κθ > ξ²) is not imposed as a constraint, since it is typically violated by market-implied parameters; instead, the simulation scheme is designed to handle variance paths that reach zero.

## 4.3 Numerical Implementation

Exotic payoffs are priced by Monte Carlo simulation using the Quadratic-Exponential (QE) scheme of Andersen (2008) for the variance process, which handles the boundary behaviour at zero without the bias of full-truncation Euler schemes. The default configuration uses 100,000 paths with antithetic variates and daily time steps. Greeks are computed by bump-and-revalue with common random numbers.

## 4.4 Key Assumptions and Limitations

- The model assumes constant parameters over the life of the trade. Term-structure effects in the volatility surface are only partially captured, which can lead to calibration errors for long-dated maturities.
- Short-dated skew is typically underfitted, since the Heston model cannot generate the steep short-maturity skew observed in index options without extreme parameter values.
- Interest rates are deterministic; the model is therefore not suitable for long-dated hybrid products where rate–equity correlation is material.

These limitations are addressed through the model reserve framework described in Section 7.
