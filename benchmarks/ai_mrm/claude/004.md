## 6. Outcomes Analysis and Back-testing

### 6.1 Approach

We back-tested the 1-day 99% historical simulation VaR at both the firm-wide and desk levels over the 250 business days ending 30 June 2024. Back-testing was performed against both hypothetical P&L (positions held constant) and actual P&L, consistent with the requirements of the internal models approach.

### 6.2 Firm-wide Results

| Measure | Exceptions | Basel Zone | Kupiec POF p-value | Christoffersen Independence p-value |
|---------|-----------|------------|--------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Hypothetical P&L | 4 | Green | 0.62 | 0.71 |
| Actual P&L | 6 | Yellow | 0.13 | 0.48 |

Hypothetical P&L back-testing produced 4 exceptions against an expectation of 2.5, which is within the green zone. Actual P&L produced 6 exceptions, placing the model in the yellow zone. The Kupiec test does not reject correct unconditional coverage at the 5% level for either series, and the Christoffersen test does not indicate clustering of exceptions.

Five of the six actual P&L exceptions occurred between 3 and 9 August 2024, coinciding with the yen carry-trade unwind. Analysis attributes three of these to intraday trading and fee income that are excluded from hypothetical P&L, and two to a basis widening between JGB futures and cash bonds that the model captures only through a single proxy risk factor.

### 6.3 Desk-level Results

Of the 14 desks in scope, 12 were in the green zone. The Rates Options desk recorded 7 hypothetical exceptions and the EM Credit desk recorded 6. For Rates Options, the exceptions are concentrated in periods of large moves in the swaption volatility surface and are linked to the use of ATM-only vega shocks (see Finding 4). For EM Credit, three exceptions coincide with sovereign spread moves in names with fewer than 200 days of usable history, where proxies are used.

### 6.4 P&L Attribution

Spearman correlation between risk-theoretical P&L and hypothetical P&L averaged 0.84 across desks; the KS distance averaged 0.07. Rates Options failed the Spearman threshold (0.68 against an amber threshold of 0.70), which is consistent with the back-testing results above.

### 6.5 Conclusion

Firm-wide back-testing performance is acceptable. Desk-level results identify two specific weaknesses—volatility surface representation and proxy usage for short-history EM names—which are raised as Findings 4 and 5 respectively.
