## Loan Loss Model for First-Lien Residential Mortgages

### Model Structure

The model projects losses on domestic first-lien residential mortgages held in banks' portfolios. Losses are estimated at the loan level by modelling the probability of transitions between delinquency states and the loss severity conditional on default. Projected losses for each bank are the sum of losses across all loans in the bank's portfolio over the nine-quarter projection horizon.

The model consists of three components:

1. A transition model that estimates the quarterly probability that a loan moves between current, delinquent, default, and prepaid states.
2. A loss severity model that estimates the loss, as a share of the outstanding balance, given that a loan defaults.
3. An exposure model that projects the outstanding balance of each loan over the projection horizon.

### Transition Model

Transitions are modelled using a series of multinomial logit regressions, estimated separately for loans in each delinquency state. The explanatory variables include loan characteristics at origination, such as the original loan-to-value ratio, borrower credit score, and loan purpose; dynamic loan characteristics, such as the current loan-to-value ratio, which is updated using projected house prices; and macroeconomic variables, including the unemployment rate and interest rates.

The model is estimated using historical loan-level data covering a full economic cycle, including the period of elevated defaults following the 2007–2009 financial crisis. This ensures that the estimated relationships reflect the behaviour of mortgage borrowers under stressed conditions.

### Loss Severity Model

Loss severity is modelled as a function of the current loan-to-value ratio at the time of default, the geographic location of the property, and the projected change in house prices between default and liquidation. The model accounts for the time between default and liquidation, during which foreclosure costs accumulate and property values may decline further.

### Treatment of Bank-Specific Factors

The model applies the same estimated relationships to all banks. Differences in projected losses across banks reflect differences in the characteristics of their portfolios rather than bank-specific parameters. This approach is consistent with the principle that supervisory models should produce results that are comparable across firms and do not depend on firms' own historical experience.

### Key Assumptions

The model assumes that the relationships between loan characteristics, macroeconomic conditions, and loan performance observed in the historical data will continue to hold under the stress scenario. The model does not explicitly account for changes in servicing practices or government programs that may affect loan outcomes, such as loan modification or forbearance programs.
