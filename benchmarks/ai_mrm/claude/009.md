# Gap Analysis: Model Risk Management Framework vs PRA SS1/23

## 1. Introduction

The Prudential Regulation Authority's Supervisory Statement SS1/23, *Model risk management principles for banks*, took effect on 17 May 2024 for firms with internal model approval. It sets out five principles that together define the PRA's expectations for an effective model risk management (MRM) framework. This document assesses the Bank's current framework against each principle, identifies gaps, and proposes remediation actions.

The assessment is based on a review of the Model Risk Policy (v4.2), the Model Validation Standard, the model inventory, and interviews with key stakeholders across the first and second lines.

## 2. Summary of Results

| Principle | Description | Assessment |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| 1 | Model identification and model risk classification | Partially compliant |
| 2 | Governance | Largely compliant |
| 3 | Model development, implementation and use | Largely compliant |
| 4 | Independent model validation | Compliant |
| 5 | Model risk mitigants | Partially compliant |

## 3. Detailed Assessment

### Principle 1: Model Identification and Model Risk Classification

**Current state:** The Bank maintains a model inventory of approximately 340 models, with tiering based on materiality and complexity.

**Gaps identified:**
- The model definition does not explicitly cover deterministic quantitative methods (DQMs) such as spreadsheet-based calculators, which SS1/23 brings into scope for certain governance requirements.
- The inventory does not record model interdependencies, making it difficult to assess aggregate model risk.
- Tiering does not consider model complexity attributes specific to AI/ML models, such as explainability and dynamic recalibration.

### Principle 2: Governance

**Current state:** The Board Risk Committee has oversight of model risk, and the CRO has been designated as the SMF responsible for the MRM framework.

**Gaps identified:**
- Model risk appetite is defined qualitatively. SS1/23 expects firms to set a model risk appetite with measurable metrics.
- Board reporting on aggregate model risk is limited to counts of open findings.

### Principle 3: Model Development, Implementation and Use

**Current state:** Development standards are well established for credit risk models.

**Gaps identified:**
- Development standards for market risk and treasury models are less detailed.
- Testing standards for model implementation (e.g., parallel runs, reconciliation of production outputs to development code) are not consistently applied.

### Principle 4: Independent Model Validation

**Current state:** The Model Validation function is independent and adequately resourced. No significant gaps were identified.

### Principle 5: Model Risk Mitigants

**Current state:** Post-model adjustments are used, and a process exists for approving them.

**Gaps identified:**
- There is no consistent framework for quantifying and reporting the impact of post-model adjustments.
- Restrictions on the use of models pending validation are not consistently tracked.

## 4. Remediation Plan

The gaps identified above will be addressed through a remediation programme overseen by the Model Risk Committee. Priority will be given to the definition of model risk appetite and the extension of the inventory to DQMs, as these represent the most significant areas of non-compliance.
