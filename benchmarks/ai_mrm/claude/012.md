## 7. Sensitivity Analysis and Benchmarking

### 7.1 Objectives

The purpose of this section is to assess the stability of the SABR model's outputs to changes in inputs and parameters, and to compare its performance against alternative modelling approaches. These tests help identify areas where the model may be sensitive to assumptions and inform the assessment of model uncertainty.

### 7.2 Parameter Sensitivity

The SABR model is characterised by four parameters: α (initial volatility), β (CEV exponent), ρ (correlation), and ν (volatility of volatility). In the Bank's implementation, β is fixed at 0.5 across all expiries and tenors, while α, ρ, and ν are calibrated to market quotes.

We tested the impact of alternative choices for β on the calibrated smile and on the valuation of the CMS and swaption portfolios:

| β | Average calibration RMSE (bp normal vol) | Change in CMS portfolio PV |
|---|------------------------------------------|----------------------------|
| 0.0 | 0.41 | +$2.1m |
| 0.25 | 0.38 | +$0.9m |
| 0.5 (current) | 0.39 | — |
| 0.75 | 0.52 | −$1.4m |

The choice of β has limited impact on the fit to vanilla swaptions, as expected given the well-known redundancy between β and ρ. However, it has a more pronounced effect on CMS valuations, which depend on the wings of the smile. This confirms that β is a meaningful source of model uncertainty for wing-sensitive products.

### 7.3 Input Sensitivity

We shocked market inputs to assess the stability of the calibration:

- **Quote perturbation:** Adding random noise of ±0.5bp to input volatilities led to changes in calibrated ν of up to 15% for short expiries, indicating some instability at the front end of the surface.
- **Negative rates:** For low-rate currencies, the shifted SABR implementation with a 2% shift performed adequately. However, results are sensitive to the choice of shift, and the documentation does not justify the selected value.

### 7.4 Benchmarking

We compared the model against two alternative approaches:

1. **Free-boundary SABR**, which handles negative rates without an arbitrary shift.
2. **SABR with the Antonov–Konikov–Spector exact density**, which removes the arbitrage issues of the Hagan approximation in the low-strike wings.

For standard swaptions near the money, all three approaches produced prices within 0.3bp of normal volatility. Differences increased significantly in the low-strike wings, where the Hagan approximation produced negative densities for 6 of 40 expiry–tenor pairs tested, compared to none for the exact density approach.

### 7.5 Conclusions

The model performs well for near-the-money swaptions, but the analysis highlights two areas requiring attention: the sensitivity of wing-dependent products to the choice of β and shift, and the presence of negative densities in the Hagan approximation. These are raised as Findings 5 and 6.
