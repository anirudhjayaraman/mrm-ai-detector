MEMORANDUM

To: Model Risk Committee
From: Model Validation — Stress Testing
Date: 14 November 2024
Re: Annual review — Net Interest Income PPNR model (MRM-ST-0112)

Recommendation: Continue use. No change to the model rating (Satisfactory with findings).

Background

This is the annual review of the NII component of our PPNR suite. The model projects net interest income for the consumer deposit and card portfolios over the nine-quarter CCAR horizon using balance-weighted rate regressions. It was last fully validated in Q4 2022; the next full validation is due Q4 2025.

What we looked at

We reviewed the 2024 ongoing monitoring pack, the two minor changes made this year (a recalibration of the deposit beta for savings products, and an update to the prepayment speed input), the status of open findings, and how the model held up during the 2024 cycle.

What we found

Performance was mostly fine. Quarterly projections against actuals were within 4% for the last six quarters, which is better than the 2023 review. The one exception is the money market savings segment, where the model under-projected interest expense by about 11% in Q1 and Q2 2024. This is the same deposit-beta problem flagged in Finding 2022-03: the model was fitted on a period of low rates and it's still catching up with how quickly customers moved balances once rates went up. The beta recalibration in June helped but didn't fully close the gap.

Both changes this year were reviewed by us at the time and classed as non-material. We agree with that.

Open findings: two remain open. Finding 2022-03 (deposit beta) is now 9 months past its original due date. The developers' plan is to move to a nonlinear beta specification for the 2025 cycle. We think that's the right fix but we're not comfortable letting it slide again — we're asking the committee to hold them to the 31 March 2025 date. Finding 2022-05 (documentation of the management overlay) is on track.

Bottom line

The model is doing its job, but the deposit-beta issue is real and it matters more in a stress scenario where rates move fast. We'd recommend keeping the existing overlay of $45m on interest expense in place until the new specification is validated.
