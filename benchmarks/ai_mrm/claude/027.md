## 5. Key Findings: Credit Risk Models

### 5.1 Overview

The targeted review assessed 200 credit risk models across 65 institutions, covering probability of default (PD), loss given default (LGD), and credit conversion factor (CCF) models for retail and non-retail exposures. The investigations identified a total of 3,400 findings, of which approximately 30% were classified as high severity. The most common areas of deficiency related to data quality, the estimation of risk parameters, and the treatment of margins of conservatism.

### 5.2 Data Quality

Deficiencies in data quality were identified in the majority of institutions reviewed. The most frequent issues included:

- incomplete or inaccurate data for the identification of defaults, in particular where the definition of default had changed over the observation period;
- insufficient processes to ensure the representativeness of the data used for model development relative to the institution's current portfolio;
- weaknesses in the reconciliation between data used for model development and accounting or risk data.

In several cases, institutions were unable to demonstrate that historical data had been adjusted consistently following changes to internal processes or IT systems.

### 5.3 Estimation of PD

For PD models, the most common findings related to the calibration of the long-run average default rate. Many institutions did not include a sufficient number of years in the observation period, or did not demonstrate that the period selected was representative of the likely range of variability in default rates. In addition, a number of institutions applied calibration approaches that did not adequately account for the cyclicality of their rating systems.

### 5.4 Estimation of LGD

LGD models were found to be particularly affected by deficiencies relating to the treatment of incomplete recovery processes and the estimation of downturn LGD. In a significant number of cases, institutions excluded incomplete workouts from the estimation dataset without appropriate adjustment, which may result in a downward bias in LGD estimates. The quantification of downturn effects was frequently based on limited data or insufficiently justified expert judgement.

### 5.5 Margins of Conservatism

Institutions were generally found to lack a comprehensive framework for identifying and quantifying deficiencies in their estimates and applying appropriate margins of conservatism. Where margins were applied, they were often not linked to specific identified deficiencies and their size was not adequately justified.

### 5.6 Supervisory Response

Following the review, institutions were required to address the findings through remediation plans. In cases where deficiencies were considered material, supervisory measures were imposed, including limitations on the use of models and increases in risk-weighted assets.
