## 3. Evaluation of Forecast Accuracy

### 3.1 Approach

This section assesses the accuracy of the Bank's central projections for GDP growth, CPI inflation, and the unemployment rate over the period 2010–2019. We evaluate forecasts at horizons of one to eight quarters, comparing them with outturns as initially published and with the latest vintages of data. The evaluation considers three dimensions of performance: bias, efficiency, and accuracy relative to benchmark models.

### 3.2 Bias

A forecast is unbiased if, on average, forecast errors are close to zero. We test for bias by regressing forecast errors on a constant and testing whether the constant is significantly different from zero.

The results indicate that the Bank's inflation forecasts were broadly unbiased at short horizons. However, at horizons beyond four quarters, inflation forecasts tended to overpredict outturns, with an average error of 0.3 percentage points at the eight-quarter horizon. This bias is concentrated in the period 2013–2016, when inflation fell below target due to declines in energy and import prices that were not anticipated.

GDP growth forecasts showed a tendency to overpredict growth in the early part of the sample, reflecting the slow recovery from the financial crisis. Unemployment forecasts were the least accurate, with a persistent tendency to overpredict unemployment as the labour market recovered more quickly than expected.

### 3.3 Efficiency

Forecasts are efficient if they make full use of the information available at the time they are produced. We test for efficiency by examining whether forecast errors are correlated with information available at the time of the forecast, including previous forecast errors and revisions.

We find evidence of inefficiency in unemployment forecasts: errors were positively autocorrelated, suggesting that forecasters were slow to incorporate evidence of a faster-than-expected labour market recovery. There is less evidence of inefficiency in the inflation and GDP forecasts.

### 3.4 Comparison with Benchmarks

To assess whether the Bank's forecasts add value, we compare their accuracy with that of simple statistical benchmarks, including a random walk, an autoregressive model, and the average of external forecasters.

The Bank's inflation forecasts outperformed the random walk and autoregressive benchmarks at all horizons, and performed similarly to the average of external forecasters. For GDP growth, the Bank's forecasts outperformed the random walk but did not significantly outperform the autoregressive benchmark beyond four quarters. These results are consistent with the broader literature, which finds that the predictability of GDP growth declines rapidly with the forecast horizon.

### 3.5 Conclusions

Overall, the Bank's forecasts have performed reasonably well relative to benchmarks, particularly for inflation. However, the evaluation highlights two areas for improvement: the persistent overprediction of unemployment and the bias in medium-term inflation forecasts. We recommend that the Bank review the treatment of labour market dynamics in its forecasting models and strengthen its processes for learning from past forecast errors.
