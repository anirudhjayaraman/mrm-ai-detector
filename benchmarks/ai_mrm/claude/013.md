# Section 8: Model Limitations and Compensating Controls

## 8.1 Overview

All models are simplifications of reality and are subject to limitations arising from data, methodology, and implementation. This section identifies the key limitations of the Non-Maturity Deposit (NMD) Behavioural Model, assesses their potential impact on the measurement of interest rate risk in the banking book (IRRBB), and describes the compensating controls in place to mitigate them.

## 8.2 Key Limitations

### Limitation 1: Limited Experience of Rising Rate Environments

The model is estimated on data from 2010 to 2022, a period dominated by historically low and stable interest rates. As a result, the estimated deposit betas and decay rates may not accurately reflect customer behaviour during a period of rapidly rising rates, when depositors are more likely to move balances to higher-yielding products.

**Potential impact:** High. Underestimating deposit outflows or pass-through rates could lead to an overstatement of the duration of the core deposit base and an understatement of earnings at risk.

**Compensating controls:**
- The model output is subject to a management overlay that caps the core proportion of savings balances at 70%.
- Stress scenarios include a deposit run-off assumption calibrated to observed outflows in the March 2023 regional banking stress.
- Deposit betas are reviewed quarterly by ALCO against observed pass-through.

### Limitation 2: Segmentation Granularity

The model segments deposits into four product types but does not distinguish between customer behaviour by channel (digital versus branch) or by balance band.

**Potential impact:** Medium. Digital-only customers and high-balance customers are likely to be more rate-sensitive than the segment average.

**Compensating controls:**
- Balance concentration is monitored monthly, with escalation to ALCO if balances above £250,000 exceed 15% of a segment.
- A model enhancement to introduce balance-band segmentation is planned for 2025.

### Limitation 3: Competitor Pricing

The model does not include competitor pricing as an explanatory variable due to data availability constraints.

**Potential impact:** Medium. Changes in competitor rates are a key driver of deposit migration.

**Compensating controls:**
- The Treasury pricing team monitors competitor rates weekly and reports material divergence to ALCO.
- Expert judgement overlays may be applied where competitor behaviour diverges materially from historical patterns.

### Limitation 4: Linear Beta Assumption

The model assumes a constant deposit beta across rate levels, whereas empirical evidence suggests that betas increase as rates rise.

**Potential impact:** Medium to high in rising-rate scenarios.

**Compensating controls:**
- Sensitivity analysis using rate-dependent betas is performed and reported alongside the base results.

## 8.3 Governance of Limitations

All limitations are recorded in the model inventory and reviewed at each annual model review. Model Risk Management is responsible for assessing whether the compensating controls remain adequate, and the model owner is responsible for ensuring that the controls operate effectively.
