3. Conceptual soundness

3.1 What the model does

The card-not-present fraud model is a feed-forward network (3 hidden layers, 256/128/64 units, ReLU, dropout 0.2) that scores each authorisation request in real time. Inputs are 143 features: 61 transaction-level, 58 aggregated velocity features over 1h/24h/7d windows, and 24 device and merchant features. Output is a fraud score between 0 and 1; transactions above 0.83 are declined and 0.55–0.83 go to step-up authentication. Those cut-offs were set by the fraud strategy team, not the developers, and they're outside the scope of this validation except where they interact with calibration (see 3.5).

3.2 Choice of approach

The developers moved from the previous GBM to a neural net mainly to cut latency (p99 from 41ms to 18ms on the new scoring platform) rather than for accuracy. On the OOT window (Mar–May 2024) the NN has an AUC of 0.962 vs 0.958 for the GBM refit on the same data, so performance is basically a wash. We don't have a problem with the choice but it should be stated plainly in the documentation that the motivation was latency. At the moment section 2 reads as though the NN was chosen for better detection, which isn't what the numbers say.

3.3 Labels

This is the area we spent most time on. Fraud labels come from chargebacks and confirmed customer reports, and both arrive late — median 34 days, 95th percentile 87 days. The developers dropped the last 90 days of data from training to let labels mature, which is sensible. But they didn't look at the transactions that were declined by the old model. Those never get a label, so the training data is missing exactly the highest-risk population. The documentation mentions this in one sentence. We think it needs more than that: at minimum a comparison of feature distributions for declined vs approved, and some view on whether reject inference is worth doing. We've raised this as Finding 1 (High).

3.4 Features

Most features make sense. Two we questioned:

- merchant_risk_score is itself the output of another model (MDL-0877) that hasn't been validated since 2021. If that model drifts, this one inherits the problem.
- time_since_last_password_reset had a big jump in importance between the 2023 and 2024 builds. Developers traced it to a change in how the digital banking platform logs resets in Feb 2024. It's working as intended now but it's the sort of thing that should be caught by monitoring, and currently isn't.

3.5 Calibration

The raw scores aren't calibrated probabilities and aren't meant to be, but the strategy thresholds are being treated as if a 0.83 score means something stable over time. Isotonic recalibration on a rolling window would make the thresholds much less fragile. Recommendation, not a finding.
