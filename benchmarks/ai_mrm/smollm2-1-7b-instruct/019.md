Data Quality and Representativeness Assessment

1. Introduction
   This section aims to evaluate the overall quality and representativeness of the dataset used for training the Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) model that forms the basis of the Credit Card Application Scorecard. Ensuring high-quality data and representing the population accurately is crucial for reliable and unbiased results.

2. Overview of Data Collection
   The dataset was sourced from a well-established financial institution with a strong history of providing quality services. The collection process involved multiple stages: 

   a. Customer information database integration: Integrations were established with various customer information databases managed by the bank.

   b. Fraudulent activity detection: An automated system for detecting fraudulent activities was integrated into the system to identify potential cases of fraud.

3. Exploratory Data Analysis
   Through exploratory data analysis, several issues concerning data quality emerged. Some common observations include:

   a. Missing Values: A significant amount of missing values occurred in both numerical and categorical fields. The majority of these missing values were found in the 'CreditScore' field.

   b. Outliers: A few outliers appeared in certain numeric fields like 'Income'. These outliers could potentially distort the model's predictions if not addressed properly.

   c. Data Imbalance: The dataset exhibited an imbalanced distribution, where there were significantly more negative than positive outcomes when predicting delinquent accounts.

4. Handling Missing Values and Outliers
   To address the issue of missing values, we applied imputation techniques such as mean imputation and regression imputation using the 'CreditScore' variable. For the outliers, we first identified them through statistical methods and visualizations. We then followed up by applying appropriate outlier handling strategies based on their nature - either removing or transforming them depending on whether they were errors or anomalies.

5. Data Preprocessing and Feature Engineering
   After addressing the aforementioned concerns, the next step was preprocessing the data by encoding categorical variables appropriately and scaling numeric features according to standard distributions. Feature engineering techniques such as polynomial transformations were also employed to capture non-linear relationships within the data.

6. Sampling Methods
   For further improving the representation of underrepresented groups within the sample, stratified sampling was used. This method ensured that each subgroup had an equal chance of selection, thereby preventing any one group from dominating the representation too much.

7. Verifying Data Completeness and Integrity
   Following the completion of all data preprocessing steps, it was essential to verify that all steps taken did not introduce any false positives or biases. This was achieved through thorough cross-validation and comparing the performance metrics of different models run over various subsamples.

8. Conclusion
   In conclusion, this assessment highlights the importance of comprehensive quality checks during the initial stages of data collection. By implementing rigorous data cleaning processes and ensuring robustness in data preprocessing and feature engineering, the GBM model obtained here has been proven to provide accurate representations of the underlying patterns in the data, enhancing its reliability for lending decisions.
