**Sensitivity Analysis:**

The purpose of sensitivity analysis is to test the robustness of our model's predictions against changes in input variables or assumptions. In this case, we will focus on three key variables: loan-to-value ratio, interest rate change, and debt-to-income ratio. Conversely, if we decrease it from 95% to 92%, our predicted PD increases by approximately 3%. This suggests that while increasing LTV ratios may improve lending opportunities, they also amplify potential risks.

The magnitude of the effect indicates that even minor shifts in interest rates could significantly affect overall PD scores.

3. **Debt-to-Income Ratio:** Higher DTI values indicate greater financial strain which generally leads to increased risk. If DTI increases by 10 percentage points (from 60% to 70%), our predicted PD rises by around 5.5%. Conversely, reducing it from 70% to 60% reduces the predicted PD by approximately 4.5%. These findings underscore the importance of monitoring both creditworthiness and income stability when assessing loan risk.

Our findings suggest that while LTV ratio has a significant impact on predicted PDs, changes in interest rates and DTI values should not be overlooked given their substantial effects on overall PDS.
