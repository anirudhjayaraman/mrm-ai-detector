### Methodology Section: Commercial Real Estate Default Model Development

**Introduction**

This document outlines the methodology used to develop the commercial real estate default model, providing insight into data sources, inputs, algorithms, and outputs. The purpose is to ensure clarity in understanding the framework behind the model and its components.

#### Data Sources

1. **Historical Financial Data**: Publicly available financial reports from commercial real estate companies such as Form 10-K and Form 10-Q filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). These reports provide detailed information on financial performance, such as income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements.

2. **Market Research Reports**: Industry publications and market research reports, offering insights into economic trends, property valuations, interest rates, and occupancy rates. These reports help establish a baseline for our analysis and provide context for predictions.

3. **Regulatory Data**: Information derived from regulatory bodies like the Federal Reserve and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), which regulate banks and banking institutions that may be involved in lending activities related to commercial properties. This data provides critical input on creditworthiness metrics.

#### Inputs

1. **Property Characteristics**: Detailed descriptions of each property's characteristics including physical attributes, location, type of property, lease terms, and rental capacity.

2. **Economic Indicators**: Variables such as GDP growth rate, unemployment rate, average rent increase per annum, and property appreciation rates.

3. **Credit Scoring Metrics**: Ratings from rating agencies assessing the creditworthiness of individual borrowers or the overall creditworthiness of specific industries or sectors.

#### Outputs

1. **Default Probability**: Estimated probability of default based on historical data and current economic conditions.

2. **Loss Given Default (LGD)**: The maximum potential loss expected if a borrower defaults.

3. **Recovery Rate**: The percentage of recoverable assets upon the sale of a defaulted property.

**Algorithms**

The developed models use statistical techniques and machine learning algorithms to forecast future loan defaults. Key steps involve:

* **Data Preprocessing**: Handling missing values and standardizing variables for appropriate usage within the models.
* **Feature Engineering**: Creating new features from existing ones to enhance predictive power of the algorithm.
* **Model Selection**: Choosing suitable regression algorithms, such as linear regression, decision trees, or neural networks, based on the complexity of the problem and the availability of relevant data.
* **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Adjusting hyperparameters to optimize model performance and generalizability.
* **Cross-Validation**: Testing multiple iterations of the chosen model to ensure it performs well on unseen data and minimize overfitting.
