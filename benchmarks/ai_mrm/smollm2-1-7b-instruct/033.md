Validation Finding: Model Validation on Expected Credit Losses Using Macroeconomic Scenarios (IFRS 9)

**Model:** Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model using macroeconomic scenario data under IFRS 9 for bank asset portfolios.

**Issue:** The ECL model fails to validate correctly under different economic conditions, leading to inaccurate risk assessments and potential regulatory issues.

**Description of the Problem:**
The ECL model does not consider macroeconomic factors like inflation rates, GDP growth, unemployment rates, and interest rate changes accurately. These variables significantly affect credit losses and are crucial for validating the model's results. However, the current implementation neglects these factors and thus leads to less reliable results than anticipated.

**Consequences:**

1. **Inaccurate Risk Assessment:** The failure to account for macro-economic factors can result in misleading risk assessment, impacting the bank’s ability to manage its portfolio effectively.
   
2. **Regulatory Non-Compliance:** Regulatory requirements emphasize accurate modeling of credit risks and failure to meet this could lead to fines or legal action against the bank.

3. **Delayed Decision Making:** By providing incorrect predictions, the model may delay decision-making regarding capital allocation, resource optimization, and strategic planning.

**Recommended Remediation Actions:**

1. **Incorporate Macroeconomic Variables:** Integrate relevant macroeconomic indicators into the model to enhance accuracy and reliability. For instance, use historical relationships between inflation rates and default frequencies across similar asset types from other banks.

2. **Data Source Standardization:** Ensure that all macroeconomic data used is up-to-date, consistent and properly formatted for comparison and analysis. 

3. **Tighten Assumptions:** Regularly update and review assumptions based on new data provided by third parties such as central banks and statistical offices.

4. **Continuous Testing:** Perform regular tests ensuring that the updated model performs well during changing market conditions and remains compliant with regulatory standards.

Due Dates:

1. **Data Collection:** Within the next six weeks, gather comprehensive macroeconomic datasets including GDP growth rates, inflation rates, unemployment rates, etc.
2. **Data Modeling:** Complete updating and standardizing macro data within two months.
3. **Implementation Review:** Monitor model performance post-implementation over three months. 
4. **Continuous Testing:** Annual testing should ensure continued compliance with regulatory norms and model effectiveness in risk management practices.
