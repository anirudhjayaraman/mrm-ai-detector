Bank Model Risk Management Policy: Risk Assessment and Mitigation

1. **Introduction**

   This policy outlines the requirements for managing credit models to ensure they conform with international best practices and regulatory expectations. Credit models represent a critical component of our risk management framework, as they enable us to assess potential losses associated with specific exposures on an ongoing basis. The objective is to ensure that these models accurately reflect market conditions and provide reliable estimates for expected future outcomes.

2. **Risk Classification and Tiering of Credit Models**

   The classification of credit models based on their potential impact on the bank's business can be categorized into three tiers:
   
   - Tier 1 models represent high-risk exposures or large capital commitments. They should have higher levels of testing and monitoring to minimize potential damage in the event of errors or failures.
   
   - Tier 2 models represent moderate-risk exposures or mid-sized capital commitments. These models typically offer better cost efficiency compared to Tier 1 models but still require rigorous testing and monitoring.
   
   - Tier 3 models represent low-risk exposures or small capital commitments. These models may involve less stringent testing and monitoring than Tier 2 models due to their lower potential impact on the bank's operations.

   Each tier will have its own set of requirements related to model design, validation, maintenance, and documentation.

3. **Model Validation Frequency**

   The bank mandates regular validation for all credit models every six months. If the exposure grows larger beyond 10% of Total System Capital (TSC), the validation frequency is moved up to quarterly. If the TSC falls below 2%, then the validation period remains at one year after the last model change.

Model validation involves both statistical performance checks such as mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared percentage error (RMSPE), and qualitative assessment regarding fit and interpretability. Any non-conforming results necessitate immediate investigation and corrective actions as needed.

4. **Maintenance Requirements**

   All models must undergo continuous revision throughout their lifecycle. This includes updating parameters when relevant market factors change significantly, recalculating forecasts using new input data, and incorporating external updates from industry reports or other reliable sources. Such changes should be reported promptly to stakeholders and documented for auditing purposes.

5. **Documentation and Reporting**

   Comprehensive records of all modifications made to any credit model, along with justifications for those changes, need to be maintained. These records should include details on any revisions, additions, or removals of assumptions or methodologies used within the model. Furthermore, outputs generated directly from modified models must also be re-evaluated and re-analysed during this process.

6. **Audit and Compliance Procedures**

   Regular audits conducted by internal auditors are designed to check whether appropriate controls exist to ensure proper functioning of the models and adherence to specified standards. Compliance officers conduct regular reviews to ensure compliance with applicable laws, regulations, and operational policies governing use of the models.

In conclusion, ensuring accurate and robust credit modeling forms a crucial part of our risk management strategy. By implementing the guidelines outlined above, we aim to maintain high standards of risk management while providing valuable insights into expected financial outcomes.
