Title: Gap Analysis of Bank’s Model Risk Framework Against PRA SS1/23: Stochastic Volatility Model (Heston)

1. Introduction

The purpose of this gap analysis is to evaluate the extent to which the bank’s current model risk framework aligns with the provisions and requirements as outlined in Schedule S of Standard Supervisory Practice (SS) No. 1/23 (SS1/23) under the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA). The specific focus will be on the use of the Heston stochastic-volatility model to price equity exotics. 

2. Overview of PRA SSP No. 1/23

3. Heston Stochastic-Volatility Model

a) Description: The Heston stochastic volatility model is an extension of the Black-Scholes-Merton option pricing model that incorporates stochastic volatility. It models asset prices through a stochastic differential equation.

b) Application: The Heston model is commonly applied when there is significant uncertainty or skewness in the underlying asset returns. It has been widely adopted due to its ability to capture both constant volatility and non-constant volatility.

4. Gap Identification

5. Subgap 1: Regulatory Treatment 

a) Regulatory treatment requires banks to maintain a regulatory capital buffer proportional to their expected loss at default (LAD), often referred to as the Value-at-Risk (VaR).

b) Current model risk framework may not account adequately for potential changes in LAD resulting from modifications made to the model. The framework should ensure that changes in the parameters of the model lead to adequate adjustments in the VaR calculation.

6. Subgap 2: Quantification of Uncertainty

a) The Heston model provides an estimate of uncertainty via the standard deviation of the variance term. However, the current framework does not comprehensively quantify these risks, thereby missing opportunities to better manage them.

b) To accurately quantify the impact of stochastic volatility, more sophisticated methods beyond simple square root rules should be employed.

7. Subgap 3: Model Validation

a) The current framework might lack sufficient validation procedures to confirm the accuracy and reliability of the Heston model. Regular validation is necessary to prevent model drift over time.

b) This should include stress testing under extreme scenarios, to assess whether the current risk management practices remain robust during such conditions.

8. Subgap 4: Documentation and Communication

a) There exists room for improvement in documenting and communicating the use of the Heston model within the organization. Both internal stakeholders and external auditors need clear information regarding the usage of stochastic models to facilitate more effective monitoring and assessment.

b) Effective communication ensures transparency among all involved parties, supporting comprehensive model risk oversight.

9. Conclusion

While the Heston model offers advantages particularly for exotic options, the existing model risk framework does not fully address the complexities associated with stochastic volatility models. The recommendations provided above aim to improve the alignment between the bank’s current approach and the standards prescribed in SS1/23, enhancing overall model risk management practices.
