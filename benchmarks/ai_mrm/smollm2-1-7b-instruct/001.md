**Sensitivity Analysis and Benchmarking for Interest Rate Risk Assessment in the Banking Book Deposit Behaviour Model**

This section presents a comprehensive review of sensitivity analyses and benchmarking exercises conducted to assess the impact of potential changes on the IRRBB deposit behaviour model. The primary objectives of this exercise were:

1. **Assessing the robustness of our model**: By applying different scenarios that might affect the interest rates, we aimed to ascertain whether the model is resilient under various economic conditions.

2. **Identifying critical drivers of deposits**: Through sensitivity analysis, it was revealed which variables have the most significant influence on deposits in the model. This information will be crucial for making adjustments or improvements to the model as needed.

3. **Benchmarking performance**: Comparisons with industry standards and peer banks enable us to evaluate our bank's performance against similar entities and identify areas where we can enhance efficiency and effectiveness.

### Sensitivity Analyses

To conduct these analyses, we employed Monte Carlo simulations based on historical data and market trends, leveraging a stress test framework to simulate extreme scenarios likely to occur within specific time frames. These included but were not limited to fluctuations in key monetary policies, major macroeconomic indicators such as GDP growth rate and inflation, and shifts in credit spreads and market liquidity levels.

#### Scenario 1: Increase in Base Rates

- Increased base rates could lead to higher borrowing costs, potentially reducing demand for deposits at our institution.
- We found that a two-percentage point increase resulted in approximately $5 million less in expected deposits during peak periods.

#### Scenario 2: Global Economic Slowdown

- Economic instability often correlates with reduced consumer spending, decreased investment activities, and lower business confidence.
- A downturn scenario led to a decrease in expected deposits by nearly $7 million due to increased borrowing costs.

#### Scenario 3: Shift in Credit Spreads

- Changes in credit spreads indicate broader market sentiment affecting lending opportunities and thus customer borrowing decisions.
- An upward shift in credit spreads resulted in approximately $8 million fewer deposits.
