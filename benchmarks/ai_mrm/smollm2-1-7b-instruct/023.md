**Model Development Documentation: Historical-Simulation Value-at-Risk (HS-VaR)**

1. **Model Description**

The HS-VaR is a statistical model designed to estimate potential losses over a specific horizon with a given confidence level, based on past market data. This model calculates an estimated VaR value representing the expected loss that is likely to occur during the specified time period with a certain probability.

2. **Key Assumptions**
   - Market efficiency: The model assumes that financial markets are efficient, meaning it would be difficult or impossible for any one individual investor to consistently beat the market through independent forecasting efforts.
   - Normality assumption: The data used in this model should follow a normal distribution. For most economic parameters, such as stock prices, returns, and volatility, this assumption holds true. However, when dealing with non-normal distributions, alternative models like the Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH) model may be more suitable.
   - Stationarity assumption: The data being modeled should be stationary, i.e., its statistical properties remain constant over time.
   - No tail dependence: The model does not account for extreme events.

3. **Compensating Controls**
   - **Data Quality**: It is essential to ensure high-quality data input, free from errors and inconsistencies. Any erroneous or missing information can lead to incorrect results, which might have critical consequences in risk management.
   - **Model Validation**: Regularly validate the model against empirical evidence to maintain its accuracy over time. This includes checking for convergence, residual analysis, sensitivity tests, and out-of-sample performance evaluations.
   - **Backtesting**: Continuously backtest the model using historical data to evaluate its predictive power and performance under various market conditions.
   - **Sensitivity Analysis**: Perform sensitivity analyses to assess how changes in assumptions, inputs, or parameters impact the model's outputs, providing insights into its robustness.
   - **Cross-validation**: Cross-validate the model on different datasets or sub-samples to check if the estimates hold up across different scenarios, helping to prevent overfitting and increase model reliability.

By incorporating these compensating controls, we can enhance our understanding of the underlying risks, improve the accuracy of the model, and promote reliable decision-making within the trading book.
