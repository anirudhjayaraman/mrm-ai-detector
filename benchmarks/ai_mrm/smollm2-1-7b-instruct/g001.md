Section: Evaluation of the PPNR Regression Model Performance

Abstract: 

This report evaluates the performance of a newly developed CCAR/DFAST pre-provision net revenue (PPNR) regression model. The model is designed to assess the credit risk assessment of loan applications and identify potential defaults based on historical data from the lending institution. The analysis examines the predictive power of the model against real-world outcomes to determine its reliability as a tool for credit risk management within the institution.

Introduction:
The primary objective of this study was to provide an independent assessment of the PPNR model's effectiveness in predicting pre-provision net revenues. This assessment aimed to identify any limitations or areas for improvement in the current model framework. Additionally, we evaluated whether the PPNR model could be utilized as part of the institution's overall credit risk assessment strategy.

Methods:
To evaluate the accuracy and reliability of the PPNR model, we analyzed a dataset of loan application data along with corresponding financial metrics. This included pre-provision net revenues over a period of five years. We then used the following steps to construct our model:

1. Data Collection: We gathered the necessary data which included information such as loan originations, loan amounts, interest rates, default rates, and respective monthly cash flow statements for each month throughout the five-year time frame.

2. Feature Engineering: In order to make more accurate predictions, additional features were added into the model. These include variables such as industry trends, economic indicators like GDP growth rate, inflation rate etc., all of which significantly influenced past loan defaults.

3. Model Selection and Training: A multi-step forward selection approach was chosen for model selection and training purposes where appropriate feature sets were selected through backward elimination and cross validation techniques to ensure optimal results.

4. Model Testing and Validation: To validate the performance of the model, it was tested using both internal and external datasets. Internal testing involved running the model with existing data, while external testing involved using historical data not used during model building.

5. Performance Metrics: Various statistical measures were calculated including R Squared value, Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), Coefficient of Determination (CoD), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). These metrics indicate how well the model performs in terms of prediction accuracy.

Results:
After thorough analysis of the data set and implementation of the PPNR model, significant positive outcomes were observed. The model demonstrated high levels of predictability across different economic conditions, thereby indicating its appropriateness for use within the institution. It also showed robust capabilities regarding identifying non-performing loans early enough so that corrective actions could be taken promptly.

Conclusion:
In conclusion, this study has found that the PPNR regression model outperforms other similar models currently being used by the institution. Its ability to accurately predict pre-provision net revenues under diverse market scenarios provides it a unique position within the context of credit risk assessment tools available today. Hence, the model can effectively contribute towards improving operational efficiency and reducing risks associated with lending operations within the institution.
